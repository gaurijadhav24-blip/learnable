Module 9

Frequently Asked Questions

Clear, empathetic answers addressing common community queries, assessment procedures, and classroom accommodations.

What is a learning disability?

A learning disability is a neurological difference affecting how an individual processes, retains, and expresses information. It may affect reading (dyslexia), writing (dysgraphia), spelling, comprehension, or mathematics (dyscalculia). It is not caused by visual, hearing, or motor disabilities, or intellectual disability.

Does having a learning difference mean low intelligence?

No. People with learning differences have average to above-average intelligence. Difficulties reflect specific cognitive processing differences rather than intellectual limits. With structured accommodations, they excel in higher education, arts, technology, and leadership.

What are the primary learning areas affected?

Common areas include reading fluency and phonics (dyslexia), written expression and handwriting coordination (dysgraphia), and arithmetic facts and numerical reasoning (dyscalculia). A student may face challenges in one area while demonstrating gifted performance in others.

Who is legally qualified to assess learning difficulties?

Formal assessments are conducted by licensed clinical psychologists, neuropsychologists, and certified special educators working in recognized clinics, hospitals, or government child development centers (such as NIEPID, AIIMS, or district medical boards).

Can this website diagnose a learning disability?

No. LearnAble is strictly an educational awareness resource. It does not provide clinical testing, formal diagnosis, or medical treatment plans. Always consult qualified healthcare or psychological professionals for diagnostic evaluation.

How can parents support a child with learning difficulties at home?

Parents can provide structured, predictable routines, break homework into manageable 20-minute chunks, use positive reinforcement, celebrate effort over marks, and actively collaborate with school teachers.

What accommodations can teachers provide in the classroom?

Teachers can provide multi-modal instruction (visual + auditory + tactile), grant 25% extra time on written tests, permit calculator or speech-to-text tools, chunk multi-step instructions, and grade concepts rather than deducting solely for spelling errors.

What legal rights and board concessions exist in India?

Under the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (RPwD) Act 2016, recognized boards such as CBSE, ICSE, and State Boards provide statutory provisions including compensatory time, exemption from a second/third language, provision of a reader/scribe, and permission to use calculators.
